## Six core destination stories

### 1. Hunza: Baltit & Altit

Label: **Fort paths & orchard villages**

Card copy: **Above Karimabad, Baltit Fort holds the memory of Hunza's rulers. Nearby, Altit's stone and timber tell another chapter. Walk between heritage and everyday life, with time for village lanes and the people caring for them.**

Facts: Baltit is more than 700 years old and now functions as a museum/cultural centre. Altit stands on a cliff in Hunza and uses stone and timber construction. Both were conserved through AKTC's community-focused work.

Suggested SVG: low rectangular timber fortress with a projecting balcony, pitched upper roof and mountain line. Do not copy a generic European castle with crenellations.

Sources:

- https://e-tourism.visitgilgitbaltistan.gov.pk/tourist-destinations/baltit-fort
- https://e-tourism.visitgilgitbaltistan.gov.pk/tourist-destinations/altit-fort
- https://www-h5.the.akdn/en/where-we-work/south-asia/pakistan/conservation-and-development-in-gilgit-baltistan-pakistan

### 2. Shigar

Label: **Carved timber & Balti heritage**

Card copy: **In Shigar, a palace rises from the rock above fields and village lanes. Fong Khar's restored rooms bring Balti building traditions close: stone, earth and beautifully worked timber, cared for through a new chapter of use.**

Facts: Shigar Fort is called Fong Khar, or Palace on the Rock; dates to the 17th century; restored with traditional materials; used as a heritage hotel with museum space.

Suggested SVG: stepped stone base, timber facade and projecting square window; use squared construction lines, not a domed palace.

Source: https://e-tourism.visitgilgitbaltistan.gov.pk/tourist-destinations/shigar-fort

### 3. Khaplu

Label: **Palace courtyards & valley life**

Card copy: **Follow the Shyok toward Khaplu, where palace courtyards and wooden architecture sit within a living Balti town. Leave space between the landmarks for a shared meal, a conversation and the rhythm of the valley.**

Facts: Khaplu is in Ghanche on the Shyok River. Khaplu Palace and Chaqchan Mosque are major landmarks. Balti cuisine highlighted by GB Tourism includes mamtu, prapu and butter tea. AKDN dates the palace to 1840.

Suggested SVG: broad symmetrical timber palace facade with central upper balcony; use a cup SVG separately for food/culture.

Sources:

- https://e-tourism.visitgilgitbaltistan.gov.pk/tourist-destinations/khaplu-valley
- https://www-h5.the.akdn/en/where-we-work/south-asia/pakistan/conservation-and-development-in-gilgit-baltistan-pakistan

### 4. Fairy Meadows / Joot

Label: **A meadow beneath Nanga Parbat**

Card copy: **Known locally as Joot, Fairy Meadows opens toward Nanga Parbat's north face. The approach is part of the experience: a locally operated mountain-road journey, then a walk into meadows where the scale of the Himalaya becomes unmistakable.**

Facts: Located in Diamer, at the Raikot side of Nanga Parbat. Access involves a steep jeep track and an onward hike. It is not a casual roadside viewpoint. GB Tourism broadly suggests May–September, subject to conditions. Do not repeat its unverified claim about WHO deadliest-road rankings or promise a fixed trek time.

Suggested SVG: three uneven Nanga Parbat-style peaks with a small meadow contour and pine, rather than Everest-like pointed icon only.

Sources:

- https://e-tourism.visitgilgitbaltistan.gov.pk/tourist-destinations/fairymeadows
- https://fwegb.gov.pk/nanga-parbat-national-park/

### 5. Phander

Label: **River bends & a slower rhythm**

Card copy: **In Phander, the river loosens into broad bends and the lake mirrors the surrounding slopes. Farmland, villages and water share the valley floor. Stay a little longer; the quiet details are the reason to come.**

Facts: Ghizer region, along the Gilgit–Shandur route; lake, river, fertile fields and farming are defining features. Fish only with relevant permissions; orchards and fields are private livelihoods. Shandur access is seasonal.

Suggested SVG: horizontally winding river, shallow hill and two poplar silhouettes.

Source: https://e-tourism.visitgilgitbaltistan.gov.pk/tourist-destinations/phander

### 6. Deosai

Label: **Wildflowers, wide skies & wildlife**

Card copy: **Beyond the narrow valleys, Deosai opens into an immense alpine plateau. Summer flowers and rolling grassland make space for Himalayan brown bears and other wildlife. Enter as a guest of this fragile high-altitude landscape.**

Facts: Alpine plateau between Skardu and Astore; national park established in 1993 with brown bear conservation as a key purpose; Himalayan brown bear habitat; UNESCO Tentative List, not an inscribed World Heritage Site. Summer access is seasonal. Use June–September as a broad planning window, and require local road/park/weather confirmation. No bear population number: UNESCO's submission is from 2016 and numbers may be stale.

Suggested SVG: rolling meadow, single flower and broad horizon, or a careful brown bear silhouette. Avoid snow leopard as Deosai's defining species.

Sources:

- https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6115
- https://e-tourism.visitgilgitbaltistan.gov.pk/tourist-destinations/deosai

## Optional Skardu gateway story

Heading: **Skardu: where the routes begin**

Copy: **Look out from Kharpocho toward the Indus, then return to the bazaar below. Skardu brings river, town and mountain routes together, making a thoughtful starting point for the wider stories of Baltistan.**

Facts: Kharpocho Fort is above Skardu/Indus; historic Maqpoon fort remains, attributed by Pakistan's archaeology department to Ali Sher Khan Anchan. Precise construction dates conflict across tourism sources; omit.

## Four culture & lifestyle themes

### The table tells a story

Copy: **Ask what's cooking in the valley you're visiting. In Baltistan, seek out mamtu, prapu and butter tea. Across the region, apricot and apple harvests connect orchard work with the food and preserves you'll find in local shops.**

Use a bowl/dumpling SVG or small serving cup, not generic cutlery alone. No health or longevity claims; no claim all these dishes originate only in one valley.

Sources:

- https://e-tourism.visitgilgitbaltistan.gov.pk/tourist-destinations/khaplu-valley

### Heritage in the making

Copy: **Look closely at a timber joint or a carved window: skill is part of the landscape here. At CIQAM in Hunza, a women's social enterprise carries that work into carpentry, restoration and other skilled trades. Heritage also makes room for new livelihoods.**

Use a chiselled rosette, carved panel or hand tool SVG. Don't present craft as unchanging, anonymous or only male work.

Sources:

- https://ciqam.com.pk/about-2/
- https://www-h5.the.akdn/en/where-we-work/south-asia/pakistan/conservation-and-development-in-gilgit-baltistan-pakistan

### A valley has a voice

Copy: **Gilgit-Baltistan holds many languages and musical traditions, including Shina, Balti, Burushaski, Wakhi and Khowar. At Altit's Leif Larsen Music Centre, young musicians learn instruments and create their own place in a continuing musical story.**

Use an original rubab-inspired SVG with a rounded resonator, long neck, tuning pegs and strings. Never label a generic guitar as a rubab. Don't invent translations; the language list is non-exhaustive.

Sources:

Copy: **Fields, orchards and village paths are places of work as well as beauty. Buy from growers and local makers, stay on public paths, and ask before entering a farm or photographing someone. A little attention makes a visit more generous.**

Use terraced field and apricot branch SVG. Don't describe occupied land as empty, untouched or undiscovered.

## Seasons: safe, concise website copy

- **Spring / April–May:** Blossom and new growth in many inhabited valleys. Timing changes with elevation and weather.
- **Summer / June–August:** A common window for higher meadows and seasonal routes. Check each route locally.
- **Autumn / September–October:** Harvest colours in many lower valleys; higher routes can close earlier.
- **Winter / November–March:** Snow and cold reshape travel; access and services need advance confirmation.

Site note: **These are broad seasonal patterns, not access guarantees. Confirm road, weather, park and local guidance before setting out.**

Sources for broad patterns: Baltit, Khaplu, Phander, Fairy Meadows and Deosai GB Tourism pages above. Do not display a live 'roads open' badge unless connected to a maintained official feed.

## Editorial cautions

- Represent Gilgit-Baltistan as culturally diverse; don't collapse all residents into one language, dress, cuisine or religious identity.
- Prefer living heritage and contemporary community life to 'lost world', 'simple people', 'untouched paradise' or 'living museum'.
- Never describe Baltit or Deosai as UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Conservation awards and tentative listing are different statuses.
- Use location-specific photographs and truthful alt text; do not use an Alpine/Swiss panorama labelled Hunza.
- Distinguish illustrative SVG landmark interpretations from measured architectural drawings.
- Avoid unsourced superlatives such as 'world's second highest plateau'. Use 'high alpine plateau'.
- Keep exact travel durations, dates, fees and flight timetables off the page unless maintained.
